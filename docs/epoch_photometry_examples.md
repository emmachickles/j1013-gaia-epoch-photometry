# Examples beyond white dwarfs

Selected direct uses of Gaia photometric time series, with a distinction between processing-team catalogues and later scientific applications. Citation counts below are OpenAlex counts retrieved on 2026-11-05; coverage differs from ADS and Google Scholar, and the counts are not a field-normalised impact ranking.

| Paper | Contribution from epoch photometry | OpenAlex citations |
|---|---|---:|
| [Clementini et al. (2019), DR2 Cepheids and RR Lyrae](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833374) | Processing-team characterisation using multiband time series: 140,784 RR Lyrae and 9,575 Cepheids. An established example of the broader scientific reach of Gaia variability. | [226](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833374) |
| [Rimoldini et al. (2023), DR3 all-sky classification](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245591) | Processing-team classification of 12.4 million variable-source and galaxy candidates. This number is not the number of published light curves: most galaxy-candidate time series were not released. | [114](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245591) |
| [Holl et al. (2023), scan-angle-dependent signals and spurious periods](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245353) | Uses published variable-source and GAPS photometry to explain false periods from source structure and scanning geometry. A GAPS application relevant to any period search. | [57](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245353) |
| [Evans et al. (2023), Gaia Andromeda Photometric Survey](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202244204) | Introduces the variability-unselected 5.5° field, error renormalisation and three-band variability diagnostics. A data/methods paper, not a claim that every source is in M31. | [30](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202244204) |
| [Panahi et al. (2022), detection of transiting exoplanets](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202243497) | Transit searches in Gaia photometry discovered Gaia-1b and Gaia-2b; radial velocities confirmed both as hot Jupiters, with independent TESS validation. A concrete discovery example beyond white dwarfs. | [21](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202243497) |
| [Mas et al. (2026), young-star variability](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202554522) | Analyses Gaia young-star light curves and uses GAPS alongside them to develop variability metrics. Characterises approximately 23,000 young sources, linking variability modes to accretion, extinction and rotation. This is not a study of M31 membership or a highly cited paper yet. | [1](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202554522) |

For the lightning talk, use the sky map to explain the DR3 selection, briefly mention pulsating stars and transiting planets, then show J1013 as an orbital-clock application. The Andromeda patch is particularly useful as a preview of releasing time series without a variability preselection. The cited GAPS applications concern photometric selection, systematics and young-star variability; they should not be presented as discoveries about Andromeda's stellar population.
